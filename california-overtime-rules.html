California Overtime Rules: Daily OT, Double Time & the 7th Day

Overtime Calculator →

California plays by tougher rules than federal law: 1.5x after 8 hours in a day (not just 40 in a week), double time after 12 hours, and premium pay on the 7th consecutive workday. If you work in California, the federal 40-hour rule is only half the story.

The three California triggers

WhenRate
Over 8 hours in a single day1.5x
Over 12 hours in a single day2x (double time)
Over 40 hours in the week1.5x
7th consecutive day, first 8 hours1.5x
7th consecutive day, after 8 hours2x

Per the California DIR — Overtime FAQ.

A real example: the 10-hour day

You earn $22/hour and work four 10-hour days (40 hours total). Under federal law: zero overtime — you never passed 40. In California:

  • Each day: 8 regular + 2 OT hours → 8 × $22 + 2 × $33 = $242/day
  • Four days: $968 vs. $880 at straight time
  • That's $88 extra the federal rule alone would never give you.

This is why California workers should never use a federal-only calculator. Daily thresholds catch overtime that the weekly 40-hour rule completely misses.

Who's exempt (the fine print)

California's exemptions are narrower than most states expect. The common ones: executive, administrative, and professional employees meeting both duties and salary tests (California's salary threshold is higher than the federal one — it's tied to 2x the state minimum wage for full-time work). Outside salespeople and certain tech workers have their own rules. When in doubt, the default is non-exempt — the employer has to prove the exemption, not you.

Quick answers

Does California require overtime after 8 hours even under 40/week?

Yes — that's the whole point of the daily rule. Four 10-hour days (40 total) still earns 8 overtime hours in California, where federal law would pay zero.

What about the 7th day — does it have to be Sunday?

No. It's the 7th consecutive day you work in a workweek, whatever day that falls on. The workweek is defined by your employer (a fixed 7-day period).

Can my employer average my hours over two weeks to avoid OT?

No — California prohibits averaging across workweeks. Each workweek stands alone, and each day stands alone for the daily thresholds.
