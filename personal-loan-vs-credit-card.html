Personal Loan vs Credit Card: Which Costs Less?

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

For borrowing a fixed amount, a personal loan almost always costs less than a credit card. Personal loan rates are typically far lower than credit card APRs, and the fixed term forces you to actually pay it off — instead of revolving the balance for years. Credit cards win only for short-term borrowing you can repay within weeks.

The $10,000 test

Nothing settles a debate like numbers. Say you need to borrow $10,000 and you'll take about 3 years to repay it. Here's the honest comparison:

Personal loan (11% APR)Credit card (24% APR)
Monthly payment~$327~$393*
Total interest~$1,780~$4,150*
Payoff dateFixed: 36 monthsDepends on you

*Credit card estimate assumes you pay a fixed $393/month. In reality, most people pay the minimum — and minimums are designed to keep you in debt as long as possible.

The personal loan saves you roughly $2,370 in this scenario. Same borrowed amount, same three years — wildly different price tags. That's the power of the rate gap.

Why credit cards are so expensive for big borrowing

Two reasons, and they compound (pun intended):

  • The rate is brutal. Credit card APRs commonly sit in the 20s, while personal loans for decent credit land in the high single digits to low teens. You're paying double the rate or more from day one.
  • There's no finish line. A personal loan has a term — 36 months and you're done, whether you like it or not. A credit card lets you pay the minimum forever, which means the balance lingers and interest keeps compounding. The structure itself works against you.

This is also why carrying a card balance month to month is one of the most expensive habits in personal finance. If you're doing it now, our credit card interest breakdown shows exactly what it's costing you.

When the credit card actually wins

Fair is fair — cards aren't always the villain:

  • You can repay within the grace period. Pay the full balance by the due date and you pay zero interest. No loan can beat free.
  • 0% intro APR offers. A 12–18 month 0% balance transfer or purchase APR beats any loan rate — if you pay it off before the promo ends. Miss that deadline and the rate skyrockets retroactively on some offers.
  • Rewards on planned spending. Putting planned, budgeted purchases on a rewards card and paying in full is just free money. The key words are "paying in full."
  • Emergencies with no time to apply. A loan takes days; a card is instant. In a true emergency, speed matters more than optimization.

The 30-day rule: if you can't repay it within a month or two, don't put it on a credit card. Get the personal loan. Your future self is begging you.

The psychology trap nobody mentions

Here's the sneaky part: credit cards feel cheaper because the minimum payment is tiny. $10,000 at 24% might ask for just $200 a month minimum. Feels manageable! But at that pace you'd pay for over 7 years and hand over roughly $8,000+ in interest. The low minimum isn't kindness — it's a business model.

A personal loan's fixed payment looks bigger, but it's honest. It has an end date. It amortizes. Every payment actually shrinks the debt on a schedule. Boring? Yes. Cheaper? Enormously.

Quick answers

Is it smart to pay off credit cards with a personal loan?

Often yes — it's called debt consolidation, and swapping 24% card debt for an 11% loan saves serious money. The catch: you must stop running the cards back up. Otherwise you've just doubled your debt.

Which hurts my credit score more?

Neither inherently. What matters is utilization and payment history. Actually, moving card debt to a loan can help your score by lowering utilization — as long as you don't max the cards again.

Can I get a personal loan with fair credit?

Yes, though your rate will be higher than advertised. That's still usually far below credit card rates. Check our guide on improving loan eligibility first — even small score improvements can cut your rate.
