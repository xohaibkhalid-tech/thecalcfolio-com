Goods & Services fees at a glance

Getting paid through PayPal's Goods & Services option (send/receive money)? PayPal's US fee page lists it at 2.99% per sale, with no fixed fee. Pick "Goods & Services" above, or use the dedicated PayPal Goods & Services fee calculator. Here's what that means in practice:

Sale amountG&S feeYou keep
$25$0.75$24.25
$50$1.50$48.50
$100$2.99$97.01
$500$14.95$485.05

Goods & Services is PayPal's commercial payment type — it includes Purchase Protection for the buyer and Seller Protection for you. That's what the fee pays for. Friends & Family skips the fee but also skips all protection, and using it for business violates PayPal's terms. Selling through a website checkout instead? That's PayPal Checkout at 3.49% + $0.49.

Selling a $250 item: what each PayPal option really costs

Say you sell a handmade item for $250 and the buyer pays through PayPal. The fee depends on how they pay — and the gap between the cheapest and priciest option is real money:

PayPal Checkout — $9.22 fee. The buyer checks out on your site with PayPal. The fee is $250 × 3.49% + $0.49 = $9.22, so you keep $240.79. To land exactly $250 in your pocket, charge $259.55 — PayPal then takes $9.55 and you keep $250.00.

Goods & Services — $7.48 fee. The buyer sends the money directly and marks it as a purchase. The fee is $250 × 2.99% = $7.48, so you keep $242.53 — $1.74 more than Checkout. To receive $250 exactly, charge $257.71; PayPal takes $7.71.

Card through PayPal — $7.97 fee. The buyer pays by card without logging into PayPal. The fee is $250 × 2.99% + $0.49 = $7.97, so you keep $242.04. To receive $250 exactly, charge $258.21; PayPal takes $8.21.

The pattern holds at any price: Goods & Services is the cheapest of the three because it has no fixed fee, and Checkout is the priciest because it combines the highest percentage with the $0.49 fixed fee. The fee looks small next to the sale price. It looks bigger next to your profit. Enter your cost per item in the calculator above and it shows your profit after the fee — or read the table below, which assumes your cost is half the sale price (a 50% gross margin) and uses the PayPal Checkout rate:

Two things to notice. First, the $0.49 fixed fee hits hardest on small sales: on a $10 sale it eats almost 17% of your profit. Second, the fee's share of profit shrinks as the sale grows, because the fixed fee stops mattering. If your margin is thinner than 50%, the fee takes a bigger bite — and if it's fatter, a smaller one. That is why pricing to cover the fee matters most on cheap items.

PayPal costs beyond the transaction fee

The percentage on each sale isn't the only way PayPal charges sellers. These are the ones that catch people out:

ChargeUS amount
Currency conversion3%–4% above the base exchange rate
Chargeback$20 per chargeback
Dispute fee$15 (standard), $30 for high-volume sellers
Refunding a buyerNo fee to refund, but the original fee is not returned

Currency conversion is the quiet one. Sell to a buyer abroad who pays in another currency and you can lose the 1.50% international fee plus a 3–4% conversion spread. If you sell internationally often, holding a balance in that currency or pricing in your buyers' currency can cut this.

Frequently asked questions

How is the PayPal fee calculated?
PayPal takes a percentage of the total sale, plus a fixed fee on most transaction types. For PayPal Checkout in the US: fee = sale × 3.49% + $0.49. Goods & Services payments are 2.99% with no fixed fee. International sales add another 1.50%.
When are micropayments worth it?
Micropayments charge a higher percentage (4.99%) but a smaller fixed fee ($0.09 vs $0.49). Against PayPal Checkout (3.49% + $0.49) they win on sales under about $26.67. Above that, the standard rate is cheaper. Try both in the calculator and compare.
Does PayPal refund the fee if I refund a buyer?
No. PayPal doesn't charge you to issue a refund, but it keeps the fee you paid when you received the money. On a $100 Checkout sale you refund in full, you're still out $3.98. Factor this into your return policy and pricing.
What's the difference between Goods & Services and Friends & Family?
Goods & Services is for commercial sales and carries the seller fees above (with buyer/seller protection). Friends & Family is for personal transfers and is generally free from a bank/balance — but using it for business violates PayPal's terms.
How much does PayPal charge on $1,000?
Through PayPal Checkout, the fee on a $1,000 US sale is $35.39 (3.49% + $0.49), so you keep $964.61. As a Goods & Services payment it's $29.90 (2.99%), so you keep $970.10. If the buyer is outside the US, add another 1.50%, or $15.
How can I reduce PayPal fees?
Use micropayments pricing for small-ticket sales, avoid unnecessary international transactions when possible, and consider PayPal's merchant rates if your volume is high. The reverse calculator above helps you pass the exact fee through to your pricing.
