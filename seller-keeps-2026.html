What a Seller Keeps on a $100 Sale Across 8 Marketplaces in 2026

The short answer: sell the same $100 item in eight places and you keep anywhere from $96.80 (Stripe) to $80.00 (Poshmark). That's a $16.80 gap on one sale — same item, same price, different checkout. The four payment processors cluster within 78 cents of each other. The marketplaces are where the money goes.

Every fee below was checked against the platform's own fee page on 4 November 2026 — not a roundup, not last year's numbers. One sale, $100, no tricks. Here's what each platform actually takes.

How we worked this out (methodology)

Checked 4 November 2026. Every rate above comes from the platform's own fee page, linked in the table. No rate was taken from a roundup or a calculator site.

  • The sale: one item, $100, free shipping, no sales tax, US seller, standard account. No Store subscription (eBay), no paid plan (Square Plus), no promotion, no Offsite Ads (Etsy), no Boosted Listing (Depop).
  • Shipping: excluded from the $100, but the fee base matters — and it differs. eBay, Etsy and Depop all charge their percentage on the item plus shipping you charge the buyer (eBay and Depop also include sales tax in the base; Etsy's 3% processing includes tax too). So if you charge $10 shipping, eBay's fee on the same sale is $15.36, not $14.00. Whatnot's processing fee also applies to the full order including shipping and tax — only its 8% commission is charged on the item price alone. PayPal, Stripe and Square charge on the amount paid. Poshmark charges on the item price only; the buyer pays the shipping label.
  • Etsy: the $9.75 is the 6.5% transaction fee ($6.50) plus 3% + $0.25 payment processing ($3.25). The $0.20 listing fee is not counted here — you pay it when you list, not when you sell. Count it and the fee is $9.95, you keep $90.05.
  • Whatnot: standard tier — 8% commission for most categories (4% for coins) plus 2.9% + $0.30 processing. Since 21 September 2026, sellers with $15,000+ in sales over a four-week period get a lower commission tier, so high-volume sellers keep more than $88.80.
  • eBay: most categories at 13.6%. It varies a lot by category — books, movies and music are 15.3%, trading cards 13.25%, guitars and basses 6.7%, and athletic shoes over $150 are 8% with no per-order fee. Store subscribers pay less.
  • Depop: US sellers pay 0% selling commission since July 2024 — the $3.75 is payment processing only. The buyer also pays a marketplace fee of up to 5% + $1 at checkout; that's the buyer's cost, not yours, so it's not in your fee. Boosted Listings (12% on new listings since 23 March 2026) are optional and excluded here.
  • Poshmark: one flat 20% on sales of $15 or more, $2.95 flat under $15. No listing fee, no separate processing fee, and the prepaid shipping label is included — the buyer pays shipping by default.

Best and worst for a $100 sale

Best: Stripe — you keep $96.80. If you already have your own buyers — your own site, your own following — a plain payment processor is the cheapest way to take their money. Stripe beats Square by 40 cents on this sale, and it beats every marketplace by at least $6.55.

Worst: Poshmark — you keep $80.00. One flat fifth of the sale, no matter what. That's $16.80 less than Stripe on the same $100 item, and $6 less than eBay. What you get for it: no listing fee, no separate processing charge, and a prepaid shipping label in the box. For a $20 top from your closet, that trade is fine. For a $100 jacket, it's the most expensive room in this table.

The real lesson isn't "always use Stripe." Stripe and Square don't bring you buyers — you do. eBay, Etsy, Poshmark and Depop charge more because they put your item in front of people who were already shopping. The question is never just "who takes the least?" It's "who takes the least after the sale actually happens?" A $100 sale on Stripe you never make is worth $0.

That said, if you already have the buyer — a repeat customer, someone from your Instagram, a sale you arranged yourself — routing them through a marketplace checkout is just paying a finder's fee for a buyer you already found.

Quick answers

Why does eBay take so much more than Stripe?

They're selling different things. Stripe just moves money — you bring the buyer. eBay brings the buyer, handles the listing, the search traffic, and the payment, then charges 13.6% on the whole order including shipping and sales tax. That last part is the one people miss: eBay's percentage applies to more than your item price.

Is Depop really almost free?

For the seller, nearly. Depop dropped its 10% selling commission for US sellers in July 2024, so all you pay is the 3.3% + $0.45 processing fee — $3.75 on a $100 sale. The catch is on the other side: your buyer pays a marketplace fee of up to 5% + $1 at checkout, which can make your price look worse next to the same item elsewhere.

Does the $100 include shipping?

No — the table assumes free shipping and no sales tax, so the fee is on the $100 alone. If you charge shipping, eBay, Etsy and Depop take their percentage on that too, and Whatnot's processing fee covers the full order. Only Poshmark leaves shipping out of your fee entirely.

Which platform should I pick for a $100 item?

If you have your own audience, Stripe or Square — cheapest, and you keep the customer. If you need the marketplace's traffic, compare where your buyers actually are: Depop is cheapest for clothing, eBay has the biggest audience but the second-highest fee here, and Poshmark only makes sense if the built-in label and buyer pool are worth a flat 20% to you. When in doubt, cross-list and let the numbers decide.
