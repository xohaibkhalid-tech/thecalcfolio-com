How the math works

This calculator uses the same daily method your card issuer uses:

  1. Daily rate: your APR divided by 365. At 24% APR that's 0.0658% a day.
  2. Monthly interest: your balance × the daily rate × 30 days. On $3,000 at 24% that's $59.18 the first month.
  3. Month by month: interest is added to the balance, then your payment comes off. As the balance falls, so does the interest — which is why the same payment clears the card faster and faster.

It assumes no new purchases, no fees, and 30-day months, so treat the payoff date as an estimate. Our guide to calculating credit card interest walks through the daily-balance method step by step, including a worked average-daily-balance example.

3 ways to pay less interest

1. Pay a fixed amount, not the minimum

Minimum payments shrink as your balance shrinks, which stretches payoff out for years. Pick a fixed monthly payment and stick to it — see the difference with our minimum payment calculator.

2. Move the balance to a 0% card

If your credit qualifies, a balance transfer card stops interest for 12 to 21 months. Check whether the transfer fee is worth it with our balance transfer calculator.

3. Pay early in the cycle

Interest runs on your daily balance, so paying as soon as you get paid — rather than on the due date — lowers the average balance your issuer charges interest on.

Credit card interest questions, answered

What monthly interest will I pay on my card?

Your first month's interest is your balance × (APR ÷ 365) × 30. On $3,000 at 24% APR that's $59.18. As the balance falls, so does the interest — the calculator above shows the full payoff picture.

What if my monthly payment doesn't cover the interest?

Then the balance grows instead of shrinking, and the card is never paid off. The calculator will tell you when that happens — raise the payment above the monthly interest to make any progress.

Does the calculator include the grace period?

No. It assumes you already carry a balance, so interest applies from day one. If you pay the full statement balance by the due date every month, the grace period means you pay no interest on purchases at all.

Why is my result different from my statement?

Your issuer uses your actual average daily balance over the real number of days in the cycle (28 to 31), while this calculator uses 30-day months and assumes no new purchases, payments or fees. The figures should be close, not exact.

Is this financial advice?

No. This page does the math only. Your issuer's rates, fees and interest method may differ — check your statement and cardholder agreement.
