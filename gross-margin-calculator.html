Margin vs markup

Sellers mix these up constantly, and it costs money. "I mark everything up 50%" is only a 33.3% margin. Here's how the two line up:

Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin). Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup).

Don't forget marketplace fees

A margin calculated on product cost alone flatters you if a platform takes a cut of every sale. Selling a $30-cost item for $50 on eBay at roughly 13.6% + $0.40 leaves about $12.80 instead of $20, closer to a 26% margin than 40%.

Work out the fee for your platform first, then add it to the cost field above: eBay, Etsy, Mercari, PayPal or Stripe.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate gross margin?
Gross margin = (selling price − cost) ÷ selling price × 100. Sell for $50 something that cost you $30 and your gross profit is $20 and your gross margin is 40%.
What's the difference between margin and markup?
Both use the same profit, but margin divides it by the selling price while markup divides it by the cost. A $30 item sold for $50 has a 40% margin and a 66.7% markup. Margin can never reach 100%; markup can go above it.
How do I price for a 50% margin?
Divide your cost by (1 − 0.50). An item costing $20 needs a $40 price for a 50% margin. The general rule is price = cost ÷ (1 − target margin).
What is a good gross margin for an online store?
It depends on the category, so compare against sellers like you rather than a single number. What matters is that margin covers platform fees, shipping, ads and returns with something left over, which is why it's worth running your price through a fee calculator too.
Should marketplace fees count in gross margin?
For your own decisions, yes. Accounting definitions vary, but if eBay, Etsy or PayPal take 10–15% of every sale, a 30% margin before fees is closer to 15–20% in your pocket. Add fees to the cost field to see your real margin.
