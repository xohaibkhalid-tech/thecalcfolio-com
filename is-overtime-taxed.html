Is Overtime Taxed? The Real Answer

The short answer: yes — overtime pay has always been taxed like regular wages. But a federal deduction for overtime pay drew massive attention in 2025–2026, and the rules around it are exactly the kind of thing that changes. Here's what's true right now and what to verify before you file.

How overtime is taxed (the basics)

Your overtime pay lands in your paycheck as ordinary income. Federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and state taxes all apply — same as your regular hours, just more of it. There's no special "overtime tax rate." The confusion comes from withholding: a big OT paycheck can look heavily taxed because payroll systems withhold as if you earned that much every week.

Withholding ≠ your actual tax. If too much was withheld from OT-heavy paychecks, you get it back at tax time. Your W-2 tells the real story, not any single pay stub.

The "no tax on overtime" news

You've probably seen the headlines: a federal provision to let workers deduct overtime pay — specifically the half-time premium (the extra 0.5x above your regular rate) — from their taxable income. Interest exploded in early 2026, and search data shows taxpayers scrambling for answers every tax season since.

Don't file based on headlines. Tax provisions have caps, phase-outs, eligibility rules, and effective dates that news summaries skip. Before you claim anything overtime-related on your return, check current IRS guidance or talk to a tax professional. This page is information, not tax advice.

What to check on your W-2

  • Box 1 — total taxable wages (includes all overtime).
  • Box 14 — employers sometimes break out overtime or other details here; it's informational, and its labels vary by employer.
  • Your pay stubs — compare gross OT earnings vs. what was withheld to spot over-withholding early.

Quick answers

Is overtime taxed at a higher rate?

No — there's no special overtime tax bracket. It's taxed at your normal marginal rate. It only feels higher because a fat paycheck pushes withholding up for that pay period.

Can I deduct my overtime pay?

There has been a federal provision allowing a deduction for qualified overtime compensation (the premium portion). Whether it applies to you depends on the current law, caps, and your situation — verify with IRS guidance for the tax year you're filing.

Does my state tax overtime differently?

States tax overtime as ordinary income too. A few states don't tax wages at all — but that's about all wages, not overtime specifically.

General information only, not tax advice. Tax law changes frequently — consult the IRS or a qualified tax professional about your specific return.
