When does an eBay Store pay for itself?

A Basic Store costs $21.95 a month on the yearly plan ($27.95 month-to-month) and cuts the final value fee in most categories from 13.6% to 12.7%. The subscription pays for itself when the fee saving covers the monthly cost — and that point depends on what you sell, because the Store discount is bigger in some categories than others.

*On the fee saving alone: $21.95 ÷ the saving. A $100 sale in most categories saves you $0.90 with a Store; in electronics it saves $4.25. The saving only counts on the part of each sale under the Store tier cap ($2,500 per item in most categories), and it ignores the other Store perk — 1,000 free listings a month instead of 250 — which can make a Store pay off sooner if you list a lot. On the $27.95 monthly plan, the most-categories break-even is about $3,106 a month. Prices from eBay's Store selling fees page, checked November 14, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

How is the eBay final value fee calculated?
eBay takes a percentage of the total amount of the sale — that's the item price plus any shipping you charge the buyer, handling charges, and even the sales tax eBay collects. For most categories the rate is 13.6% on the first $7,500 per item, dropping to 2.35% above that, plus a $0.30–$0.40 per-order fee.
Why does eBay charge fees on shipping and sales tax?
History: when shipping was exempt, sellers listed $1 items with $30 postage to dodge fees. eBay closed that door and it stayed shut. You never receive the sales tax — eBay collects and remits it — but the fee still applies to it. Price accordingly.
Do I pay anything just to list an item?
Your first 250 listings each month are free. After that it's $0.35 per listing, whether or not the item sells. Good 'Til Cancelled listings renew every 30 days and each renewal counts against the free allowance.
My category isn't listed — what rate do I use?
Pick "Custom rate" and enter the exact percentage from your Seller Hub. Jewelry, handbags, motors parts and a few other categories have their own tiered schedules — always check the category eBay actually assigned to your listing, since two sellers with the same product can pay different rates.
How can I lower my eBay fees?
Double-check your category assignment, consider a Store subscription (Basic and above drop most categories to about 12.7%), earn Top Rated Plus status for a 10% discount on the final value fee, and only pay for Promoted Listings where the extra visibility actually converts.
What happens if my seller performance drops to Below Standard?
eBay grades sellers on the 20th of every month. If your account is rated Below Standard, eBay adds an extra 6% of the total sale on top of your normal final value fee for sales in the following month — in most categories that turns 13.6% into 19.6%. Stay Below Standard for four months in a row and it rises to 7%. You also lose search placement and access to Promoted Listings, so the penalty costs more than the extra percentage. A Very High rate of "item not as described" returns adds another 5% in those categories (6% after four months) — if both apply, eBay charges only the Below Standard one.
How much do Promoted Listings cost?
With a General campaign, you pick an ad rate between 2% and 100% of the total sale amount — and you only pay it if the promoted item sells within 30 days of a click on your ad. No sale, no fee. Since January 2026, any buyer clicking and any buyer purchasing the same item within 30 days can trigger the fee, so watch your margins on slow movers. Priority campaigns work differently: they charge per click, sale or no sale. The calculator above models General only — try a few ad rates and see what they do to your profit.
Is an eBay Store worth it?
Do the break-even math, not the marketing. A Basic Store costs $21.95 a month on the yearly plan ($27.95 month-to-month) and cuts most categories from 13.6% to 12.7%. On the fee saving alone, it pays for itself at about $2,439 a month in sales in most categories — sooner in electronics ($517) and computers ($352), where the Store discount is bigger. If you also list more than 250 items a month, the extra 750 free listings are worth something too. Below that volume, skip it. See the break-even table above.
How do I calculate eBay fees by hand?
Four steps. 1) Add the item price, the shipping you charge, and the sales tax — that is the fee base eBay charges on. 2) Multiply the fee base by your category rate (13.6% in most categories). 3) Add the per-order fee: $0.40 on orders over $10, $0.30 on orders of $10 or less. 4) Subtract the total from what the buyer paid. Example: a $50 item, no shipping, no tax — 13.6% of $50 is $6.80, plus $0.40, so eBay takes $7.20 and you keep $42.80.
