How lenders read your DTI

Back-end DTIWhat it usually means
36% or lessStrong. Within Fannie Mae's standard limit for manually underwritten loans, with room left in your budget.
37%–43%Acceptable for most mortgages. FHA's standard back-end guideline is 43%.
44%–50%Possible, usually with strong credit, savings or other compensating factors. 50% is Fannie Mae's maximum through its automated underwriting.
Over 50%Hard. Mortgage denial rates rise steeply above 50% and exceed 80% above 60%.

Sources: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-6-02; HUD FHA guidelines; St. Louis Fed, June 2026 (2018–2024 mortgage applications). Individual lenders can set stricter limits.

Front-end vs back-end DTI

Front-end DTI counts only housing: your rent or mortgage payment plus property tax, homeowners insurance and HOA dues. FHA loans look for this to be around 31% or less.

Back-end DTI counts every recurring debt, housing included. This is the number most people mean by "debt-to-income ratio," and the one lenders weigh most. If you're applying for a mortgage, put the new mortgage payment in the housing field, not your current rent, to see the ratio the lender will calculate.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good debt-to-income ratio?
Under 36% is generally considered good. That's Fannie Mae's standard limit for manually underwritten mortgages, and it leaves room in most budgets. Between 36% and 43% is workable for many loans, and above 50% most lenders will turn a mortgage application down.
Do lenders use gross or net income for DTI?
Gross income, meaning what you earn before taxes and deductions. That's why a DTI that looks fine to a lender can still feel tight in your real budget, which runs on take-home pay.
What debts count in a debt-to-income ratio?
Recurring debt payments: rent or your mortgage payment (with property tax, homeowners insurance and HOA dues), car loans, student loans, personal loans, credit card minimum payments, and court-ordered child support or alimony. Everyday bills like groceries, utilities, phone and streaming don't count.
Is 43% still the maximum DTI for a mortgage?
Not in practice. The 43% figure comes from the 2010s qualified-mortgage rules, but Fannie Mae's automated underwriting allows up to 50%, and St. Louis Fed research on 2018–2024 applications found denial rates barely move at 43% and jump sharply above 50%.
Does checking my DTI affect my credit score?
No. Your DTI isn't part of your credit report or score at all; lenders calculate it from your application. Using this calculator doesn't touch your credit.
