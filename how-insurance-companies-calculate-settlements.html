How Insurance Companies Actually Calculate Your Settlement

Most insurers start with your medical bills and lost wages (special damages), then multiply by 1.5–5x for pain and suffering (general damages). Serious, well-documented injuries get higher multipliers. Then they open low — because most people negotiate up from the first offer, not down to it.

The multiplier method, decoded

Step 1 — Special damages (the receipts): medical bills + lost wages + property damage + out-of-pocket costs. These are objective and documented.

Step 2 — General damages (the human part): pain, suffering, emotional distress, lost enjoyment of life. No receipts exist, so insurers apply a multiplier of 1.5x to 5x your medical bills.

Step 3 — Adjust: fault percentage, policy limits, and how good your documentation is.

What moves the multiplier

  • Up: fractures, surgeries, permanent impairment, long treatment, consistent medical records, visible injuries, lost work.
  • Down: gaps in treatment ("if you were hurt, why'd you skip 3 weeks?"), pre-existing conditions, low property damage, partial fault.

Tip: The single biggest multiplier-killer is inconsistent treatment. Go to every appointment. Every gap becomes the adjuster's exhibit A.

The software behind the curtain

Big insurers don't do this on napkins — they use claims software (the famous one is called Colossus) that scores hundreds of injury and treatment factors. It rewards documented, coded medical treatment and punishes vagueness. That's why "my back hurts" settles for less than "L4-L5 disc herniation with 8 weeks of PT, documented."

And the first offer? It's a starting bid, not a valuation. Industry practice is to open low and expect negotiation — which is exactly why you should never accept it before you know your number.

Quick answers

What multiplier will I get?

No one can promise a number — it depends on injury severity, documentation, jurisdiction, and the adjuster. The 1.5–5x range is the industry's working framework, not a guarantee.

Do I need a lawyer?

For minor claims with small bills, many people settle solo. For serious injuries, disputed fault, or lowball offers, attorneys typically work on contingency (no upfront cost) — and insurers take represented claims more seriously.

How long should I wait before settling?

Until you've finished treatment or reached maximum medical improvement. Once you sign, the claim closes forever — even if new bills arrive.
