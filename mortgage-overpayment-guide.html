Mortgage Overpayment: How Extra Payments Save You Thousands

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

A mortgage overpayment is any payment above your required monthly amount. Because extra payments go straight to principal, even $100 a month extra can shave years off a 30-year mortgage and save you tens of thousands in interest. The earlier you start, the bigger the payoff.

Why extra payments are so powerful

Most mortgages are amortized, which is a fancy way of saying your early payments are mostly interest. On a 30-year loan, your first years of payments barely touch the principal — you're mostly paying the bank for the privilege of owing them money. Depressing, but true.

Here's where overpaying changes the game: every extra dollar skips the interest queue and goes directly to principal. Less principal means less interest charged next month, which means more of your regular payment hits principal too. It's a snowball, but in your favor for once. The math behind this is the same amortization formula in our loan interest guide — extra principal payments just accelerate the whole schedule.

What $200 a month extra actually does

Let's make it concrete. Take a $320,000 mortgage at 6.5% over 30 years. Your normal payment is about $2,023 a month, and over 30 years you'd pay roughly $408,000 in interest alone. Yes, really — more interest than the house cost.

Now add just $200 extra per month:

Normal payments+$200/month extra
Payoff time30 years~24 years
Total interest~$408,000~$309,000
Interest saved—~$99,000

Read that again: $200 a month — the cost of a few takeout dinners — saves roughly $99,000 and frees you six years early. That's not a typo. That's amortization working for you instead of against you.

The earlier the better: extra payments in year 2 of your mortgage save dramatically more than the same payments in year 20, because early principal reductions kill the most future interest.

Three ways to overpay (pick your style)

  • Round up your payment. Payment is $2,023? Pay $2,100 or $2,200. Painless, automatic, effective.
  • One extra payment a year. Divide your monthly payment by 12 and add that chunk each month — or just make a 13th payment from a bonus or tax refund. This alone can cut roughly 4 years off a 30-year loan.
  • Lump sums when they land. Bonus, inheritance, tax refund — throwing windfalls at the mortgage beats letting them sit in a low-interest savings account, as long as your emergency fund is already solid.

Before you overpay, check these

Overpaying is usually brilliant, but not always. Run through this list first:

  • Prepayment penalties. Some mortgages charge a fee for paying extra, especially in the first few years. Check your loan terms — a penalty can wipe out the benefit.
  • Higher-interest debt first. If you're carrying credit card debt at 20%+, kill that before overpaying a 6.5% mortgage. Always attack the highest rate first.
  • Emergency fund. Don't throw every spare dollar at the mortgage and leave yourself with no cushion. Three to six months of expenses in savings comes first.
  • Retirement match. If your employer matches 401(k) contributions, grab the full match before overpaying — that's an instant 100% return you can't beat.

Also worth knowing: overpaying is different from refinancing. Refinancing replaces your loan with a new one (ideally at a lower rate); overpaying accelerates the loan you already have. Sometimes doing both makes sense — our refinancing guide covers the timing.

Quick answers

Is it better to overpay monthly or make lump sums?

Monthly overpayments win slightly because they start reducing principal immediately and consistently. But the best strategy is the one you'll actually stick with — a yearly lump sum you reliably make beats a monthly plan you abandon.

Will overpaying shorten my term or reduce my payment?

Usually it shortens your term — you keep paying the same amount but finish earlier. Some lenders let you "recast" instead, which lowers the monthly payment. Ask your servicer which options yours offers.

How much extra should I pay?

Whatever you can sustain without stress. Even $50–$100 a month makes a real dent over 30 years. Don't overpay so aggressively that you have no cash buffer — that's how people end up borrowing at worse rates later.

Can I overpay on a fixed-rate mortgage?

Most lenders allow some overpayment (often up to 10% of the balance per year) without penalty, but terms vary. Check your mortgage agreement or call your servicer before sending extra.
