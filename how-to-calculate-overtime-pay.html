How to Calculate Overtime Pay: Step by Step

Three steps: (1) find your regular hourly rate, (2) multiply it by 1.5, (3) multiply by your overtime hours. That's the whole formula — the tricky part is step 1, because your "regular rate" isn't always the number on your offer letter.

The 3 steps

Step 1 — Find your regular rate.

For hourly workers, it's your hourly wage plus the hourly value of non-discretionary bonuses, shift differentials, and commissions earned that week. Divide total straight-time earnings by total hours worked.

Step 2 — Multiply by 1.5.

Regular rate × 1.5 = your overtime rate. This is the federal minimum; your state or contract may require more.

Step 3 — Multiply by OT hours.

Overtime rate × hours over 40 (or over your state's daily threshold) = your overtime pay. Add it to your regular pay for the week.

Worked examples

Hourly worker: $18/hour, 48 hours worked.
Regular: 40 × $18 = $720. OT rate: $18 × 1.5 = $27. OT pay: 8 × $27 = $216. Total: $936.

Salaried non-exempt: $800/week salary, 50 hours worked.
Regular rate: $800 ÷ 40 = $20/hour. OT rate: $30. OT pay: 10 × $30 = $300. Total: $1,100.

With a bonus: $20/hour, 45 hours, plus a $100 production bonus that week.
Straight-time earnings: (40 × $20) + $100 = $900. Regular rate: $900 ÷ 40 = $22.50. OT rate: $33.75. OT pay: 5 × $33.75 = $168.75. Total: $1,068.75.

The bonus example is where employers mess up most. Calculated on base pay alone, that worker would get 5 × $30 = $150 in OT — $18.75 less than the law requires. Small per week, real money over a year.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using base pay instead of regular rate — bonuses and differentials count.
  • Weekly vs. daily confusion — federal law is weekly; states like California add daily thresholds on top.
  • Assuming salary = exempt — duties decide, not the pay structure.
  • Forgetting the 7th day — in California, consecutive-day rules stack on top of everything else.

Quick answers

What's the simplest overtime formula?

(Hourly rate × 1.5) × overtime hours, added to (hourly rate × 40). For a $20/hour worker with 5 OT hours: ($20 × 40) + ($30 × 5) = $950.

How do I calculate overtime from salary?

Divide weekly salary by 40 to get your regular hourly rate (for non-exempt salaried workers), then apply the 1.5x multiplier to hours over 40.

Do I count lunch breaks in overtime hours?

Only hours actually worked count. Bona fide unpaid meal breaks (30+ minutes, fully relieved of duty) don't count toward the 40 — paid rest breaks do.

General information only, not legal advice. Overtime calculations depend on your classification, state rules, and pay structure.
