Settlement FAQs

How is pain and suffering calculated?

The common method is the multiplier: your medical bills multiplied by 1.5 to 5, depending on severity. Minor injuries use ~1.5x; permanent or severe injuries up to 5x. Insurers use software with similar logic — knowing the method keeps negotiations honest.

Does being partly at fault reduce my settlement?

Yes, in most states. Under comparative negligence, your payout drops by your share of fault — 20% at fault means 20% less. A few states bar recovery entirely if you're more than 50% at fault. This calculator applies the reduction automatically.

Should I accept the insurance company's first offer?

Often not. First offers are frequently below fair value — insurers count on you not knowing the number. Run the calculator above, compare, and counter. Patience is leverage.

When should I hire a lawyer?

Serious injuries, disputed fault, or an offer far below your estimate. Represented claimants often recover more than unrepresented ones — and many personal injury lawyers work on contingency (you pay only if you win).

How long do settlements take?

Minor cases: 3–6 months. Moderate: 6–12 months. Severe: 1–2 years. Rushing usually means settling cheap.

Not legal advice. This calculator gives rough estimates for education only, using the standard multiplier method. Real settlements depend on state law, policy limits, evidence and negotiation. Talk to a licensed personal injury attorney about your case — most offer free consultations.
