How to Calculate Credit Card Interest (Without the Headache)

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Credit card interest = your average daily balance × your daily periodic rate × days in the billing cycle. A $3,000 balance at 24% APR costs you about $60 a month in interest — and that number grows every month you only pay the minimum.

The 30-second version

  • Your card company divides your APR by 365 to get a daily rate.
  • Each day, it multiplies that rate by your balance that day, then adds up all 30-ish days.
  • Pay the full statement balance by the due date and you pay zero interest on purchases. That's the grace period doing its job.
  • Pay only the minimum and the math turns brutal — most of your payment goes to interest, barely touching what you owe.

The single sentence that matters: interest is charged on your average daily balance, not your starting balance. Big purchase on day 25 of the cycle? You only pay interest on it for a few days.

Do the math with me

Say your APR is 24% and you carry a $3,000 balance through a 30-day billing cycle.

Step 1: Daily periodic rate = 24% ÷ 365 = 0.0658% per day.

Step 2: $3,000 × 0.000658 = $1.97 per day.

Step 3: $1.97 × 30 days = $59.18 in interest for the month.

Now here's where it stings: next month you're paying interest on $3,059.18. The month after, on that plus more interest. This is compounding working against you — the same force that grows savings accounts, now growing your debt.

Tip: Made a big purchase mid-cycle? Pay it off before the statement closes and it barely accrues interest at all — interest runs on daily balances, so days matter.

The grace period (use it or lose it)

Most cards give you roughly 21–25 days after the statement closes to pay in full with zero interest on purchases. But the grace period has a catch most people learn the expensive way: it only applies if you paid your previous balance in full.

Carry even $1 into the new cycle and the grace period vanishes — new purchases start accruing interest the day you make them. One leftover dollar can cost you a full month of interest on everything you buy next. If you've been carrying a balance, the only way back to free grace-period living is paying the whole thing to zero.

Also note: cash advances and balance transfers usually have no grace period at all. Interest starts ticking the second the money moves.

Why minimum payments are a trap

A $5,000 balance at 24% APR with a $125 minimum payment takes roughly 7 years to clear — and costs over $5,600 in interest alone. You'd pay more in interest than you borrowed.

Bump that payment to $200 and you're done in about 3 years with roughly $2,000 in interest. Same debt, same rate — the only thing that changed is how aggressively you attacked the principal. Minimum payments are designed to keep the account profitable for the lender, not to get you out of debt.

Quick answers

Is credit card interest charged daily or monthly?

Calculated daily, billed monthly. Your issuer applies the daily periodic rate (APR ÷ 365) to your balance each day, then totals it up on your statement.

Does paying the minimum stop interest?

No — it only keeps the account in good standing. Interest keeps accruing on the remaining balance every single day.

Why is my interest different from what I estimated?

Because it's based on your average daily balance, not the balance on any one day. Payments, purchases, and refunds during the cycle all move the number.

Do all cards use APR ÷ 365?

Nearly all US cards use 365 days. A few use 360 — check your cardholder agreement if you want the exact figure, but the difference is tiny.
