How the EMI math works

Banks don't guess your EMI — they use one fixed formula. Monthly rate r = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100, and n = total number of months:

EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1)

If the rate is 0%, it's just P ÷ n — no interest, no complication. Try it: ₹500,000 at 11% for 5 years works out to an EMI of about ₹10,871, with total interest around ₹152,273. Personal loan questions, answered straight

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you pay every month until the loan is cleared. It's "equated" because it never changes, even though the split between interest and principal shifts: early EMIs are mostly interest, later ones are mostly repayment.

How is personal loan EMI calculated?

Take the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and the total number of months. The formula is: EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the loan amount. Plug the numbers in — or just use the calculator above and skip the algebra.

Does a longer tenure always cost more?

Pretty much. A longer tenure shrinks your EMI, which feels easier — but you pay interest for more months, so the total goes up. Sometimes a lot. Always compare the total amount payable, not just the EMI, before choosing a tenure.

Will prepaying my loan save money?

Yes — if there's no prepayment penalty. Every extra payment goes straight to the principal, which cuts the interest on all future EMIs. Prepaying early in the loan saves the most, because that's when interest is at its highest. Check your loan agreement for lock-in periods or charges first.

Is this financial advice?

No. This page does math — it can't tell you whether you should take a loan, how much you can afford, or which lender to pick. For that, talk to a qualified financial advisor who knows your situation.
