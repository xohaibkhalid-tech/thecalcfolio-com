What Is a Mortgage Refinance?

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Refinancing means replacing your current mortgage with a new one — usually to get a lower rate, change the term, or pull cash out. It costs 2–5% of the loan in closing costs, so the only question that matters is the break-even: monthly savings vs. upfront cost. Example: saving $197/month with $6,000 in costs breaks even in about 30 months.

The three flavors

  • Rate-and-term: the classic — same balance, better rate or different term. Lower payment or faster payoff.
  • Cash-out: borrow more than you owe and pocket the difference. Higher balance, higher risk — you're converting equity into debt.
  • Cash-in: bring money to the table to shrink the balance. Less common, useful when you need to hit a loan-to-value target.

The break-even math (do this first)

Break-even months = closing costs ÷ monthly savings.

$300,000 loan, 30-year fixed, 7% → 6%:

  • Old payment: $1,995.91/month
  • New payment: $1,798.65/month
  • Savings: $197.26/month
  • Closing costs: $6,000 → break-even at ~30 months

Staying put for 5+ years? You come out roughly $5,800 ahead. Selling in 2 years? You lose money. The break-even doesn't care about your hopes — only your timeline.

Tip: Lenders love quoting 'no-closing-cost' refis — but the costs are usually rolled into a higher rate. Run the break-even on the rate you'd actually get, not the headline.

When refinancing is a bad idea

  • You're moving soon. If you'll sell before break-even, you're paying thousands for nothing.
  • You're resetting the clock. Refinancing a 30-year loan 10 years in means 30 more years of payments — total interest can rise even as the monthly payment falls.
  • You're stretching to cash out. Turning home equity into consumer spending is how people end up underwater twice.
  • Your credit slipped. The advertised rates assume great credit; your actual offer may not beat your current loan.

Quick answers

How much does it cost to refinance?

Typically 2–5% of the loan amount in closing costs — lender fees, appraisal, title, prepaid items. On a $300,000 loan, expect $6,000–$15,000.

How long does refinancing take?

Usually 30–45 days from application to closing, similar to a purchase mortgage. Rate locks typically last 30–60 days.

Does refinancing hurt your credit?

The hard inquiry dings a few points temporarily, and the new account lowers your average account age. Multiple mortgage inquiries within a 14–45 day window count as one for scoring purposes.
