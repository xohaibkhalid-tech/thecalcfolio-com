Top-Up Home Loan vs Personal Loan: Which Costs Less?

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: for the same amount and tenure, a top-up home loan almost always costs less than a personal loan — it runs just 0.25–1.5% above your home loan rate because your property backs it. But watch the tenure: a cheap rate stretched over 10 years can still cost more total interest than a pricey personal loan cleared in 5.

What a top-up home loan actually is

Already repaying a home loan? Most banks will lend you extra money on top of it — that's a top-up home loan. It's a separate loan, but it uses the same property as security, which is why it comes at nearly home-loan pricing instead of personal-loan pricing.

Here's how it works in practice. After a cooling-off period of 6–12 months of clean EMI payments on your existing home loan, you can apply with minimal documentation — the bank already knows you, your income, and your property. The top-up rate typically sits 0.25–1.5 percentage points above your current home loan rate. So if your home loan runs at 8.5%, the top-up lands around 8.75–10%. In October 2026, SBI's top-up loan was priced around 7.75–10.50%, depending on profile. Compare that with personal loans at 11–24% and you see the gap immediately.

How much can you get? Lenders cap the combined loan-to-value — your existing home loan plus the top-up — at roughly 65–75% of the property's current market value. If your flat has appreciated since you bought it, that headroom is what funds the top-up. Bought at ₹1 crore with a ₹75 lakh loan, property now worth ₹1.3 crore, outstanding ₹66 lakh — at a 70% LTV cap you could get roughly ₹25 lakh extra.

The purpose is flexible: renovation, a child's education, medical bills, a wedding, even consolidating expensive debt. No one asks for invoices the way some lenders do with personal loans.

Same amount, same tenure: the top-up wins by a mile

Let's put ₹10 lakh through both options, same 5-year tenure:

  • Top-up at 9.25%: EMI ≈ ₹20,877/month, total interest ≈ ₹252,620
  • Personal loan at 13.5%: EMI ≈ ₹23,011/month, total interest ≈ ₹380,660

The top-up saves you about ₹2,134 every month and roughly ₹128,000 in total interest. Same money, same repayment period, same borrower. The only difference is that your property stands behind the top-up, so the bank charges you far less for the risk.

This is the "collateral discount" at work, and it's the entire reason the comparison exists. An unsecured personal loan prices in the risk of you walking away. A top-up prices in the knowledge that the bank can recover through your property. If you already have a home loan running, ignoring the top-up option and taking a personal loan instead is genuinely leaving a lakh-plus on the table.

The tenure trap nobody warns you about

Now the plot twist. Top-up loans tempt you with long tenures — banks will happily stretch one to 10 or 15 years because the EMI looks so comfortable. And that's where the cheap rate stops being cheap.

Take that same ₹10 lakh top-up at 9.25%, but stretch it to 10 years instead of 5. EMI drops to a friendly ₹12,807 a month. Feels great. But total interest balloons to roughly ₹536,840.

Compare that with the 5-year personal loan at 13.5%: total interest ₹380,660. The "cheap" 9.25% loan just cost you about ₹156,000 more than the "expensive" 13.5% loan.

Cheapest rate does not mean cheapest loan. Rate decides the price per year; tenure decides how many years you pay it. Double the years, double (roughly) the interest — whatever the rate. This is why the golden rule is: take the top-up for the rate, but keep the tenure matched to what a personal loan would have been. If you want to see exactly how tenure inflates the total, our amortization guide breaks the mechanics down month by month.

Rule of thumb: when you take a top-up, set its tenure equal to (or shorter than) the personal loan tenure you were considering. Pocket the rate saving; don't spend it on extra years.

When a personal loan still makes sense

The top-up isn't the answer for everyone. A personal loan wins in these cases:

  • You don't have a home loan. No existing home loan, no top-up — it's tied to one. (A separate "loan against property" exists, but that's a different product with its own pricing.)
  • You need money fast. Top-ups take days to a couple of weeks — legal checks, property valuation, documentation. Personal loans from your own bank can land in hours.
  • The amount is small. For ₹1–2 lakh you can clear in a year, the paperwork of a top-up isn't worth it. Take the personal loan, clear it fast.
  • You don't want your home on the line. This is the real trade-off people skip. Default on a personal loan and your CIBIL score takes the hit. Default on a top-up and the bank can move against your property. Never secure your home for money you could repay unsecured.

Also remember: top-ups are floating-rate, so they move with the repo cycle. The RBI's October 2026 hike to 5.50% lifted top-up pricing just like it lifted home loan rates. If rates keep climbing under the "calibrated tightening" stance, a personal loan at a fixed rate could occasionally be the steadier choice — unlikely, but worth knowing.

And if the amount is modest and repayable within months, run it against the personal loan vs credit card comparison too — sometimes neither is the cheapest answer.

A quick decision checklist

Before you pick, answer these honestly:

  1. Do you have a home loan with 6–12 months of clean payments? If no, stop here — take the personal loan.
  2. Is there LTV headroom? Has your property appreciated, or have you repaid enough that outstanding + top-up stays under ~70% of current value? If the bank won't lend the amount you need, the personal loan is your fallback.
  3. Can you repay within 5 years? If yes, the top-up is almost certainly cheaper. If you genuinely need 10+ years to afford the EMI, reconsider whether you should borrow at all — or shrink the amount.
  4. Is your home worth the risk? Only borrow against your house for amounts and purposes you'd be comfortable losing sleep over. Be real with yourself here.

Yes to the first three and comfortable with the fourth? Take the top-up, match the tenure to a personal-loan timeline, and keep the ₹128,000. Our loan interest guide will help you verify the lender's numbers before you sign.

Quick answers

What is the interest rate on a home loan top-up in India?

Typically 0.25–1.5 percentage points above your existing home loan rate. In October 2026, SBI's top-up loan was priced around 7.75–10.50% p.a. depending on borrower profile — still far below the 11–24% personal loan range.

Can I get a top-up loan without a home loan?

No — a top-up is an add-on to an existing home loan with the same lender. Without a home loan, your closest alternative is a loan against property, which has different pricing and LTV norms.

Is there a tax benefit on top-up loan interest?

Only if the top-up amount is used for the home itself — purchase, construction, or renovation — and you can document it. Used for a wedding or a car, there's no tax benefit.

Does a top-up affect my existing home loan?

It's a separate loan with its own EMI and tenure, but the same property secures both. The combined loan-to-value is capped around 65–75%, so a large top-up can limit future borrowing against the property.

Are there prepayment charges on top-up loans?

Top-ups are floating-rate loans, so under the RBI's directions effective 1 January 2026, regulated lenders can't charge prepayment or foreclosure fees on them. You can prepay or foreclose free of charge.

General information only, not financial advice. Rates and LTV caps are indicative (October 2026) and vary by lender and borrower profile — always confirm with your bank.
