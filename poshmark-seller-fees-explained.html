Poshmark Seller Fees, Explained Without the Fine Print

Calculate it for your own numbersFree, no sign-up — every fee on one receipt.
Depop Fee Calculator →eBay Fee Calculator →

Poshmark takes 20% of your sale price on orders of $15 or more, or a flat $2.95 on anything under $15. No listing fees, no monthly fees, no separate payment processing charge — that one cut covers everything, including the prepaid shipping label.

The 30-second version

  • $15 and up: Poshmark keeps 20%, you keep 80%. Sell for $50, pocket $40.
  • Under $15: flat $2.95 fee, no matter the price. Sell for $8, keep $5.05.
  • $0 to list. $0 monthly. Payment processing is baked into the commission.
  • Shipping labels are prepaid and covered — the buyer pays shipping by default.

It's the simplest fee structure in resale and also the steepest percentage. Whether that trade works for you depends entirely on what you sell and how you price it.

Notice the awkward jump: at $14.99 you keep $12.04, but at $15.00 you keep $12.00. The flat fee and the percentage cross over right there — pricing at $15 exactly is the one price point that's technically worse than a penny less. Funny how that works.

Tip: Bundles count as a single sale, so one 20% cut covers the whole bundle. Three $20 items sold separately cost you $12 in fees; bundled at $60, it's the same $12 — but one shipment and one happy buyer.

The under-$15 trap

That flat $2.95 looks harmless until you do the division. On a $14.99 sale it's about 20% — fair enough. On a $10 sale it's 29.5%. On a $5 sale it's 59%. Poshmark quietly punishes cheap listings, which is why experienced sellers either bundle low-priced items or list them elsewhere.

If you're clearing out a closet full of $6–$8 tops, Poshmark is the wrong room. Bundle five of them at $30 and the math heals instantly: one $6 fee instead of five $2.95 fees.

What the 20% actually buys you

Twenty percent stings, but it isn't pure profit for Poshmark. Folded into that single fee: payment processing (other platforms charge this separately), seller protection, and the prepaid USPS shipping label system. There's genuine convenience in never touching a shipping label or a payment dispute alone.

Where it falls apart is on higher-priced items. On a $200 sale you hand over $40 — on eBay you'd keep roughly $13 more, on Depop (which dropped its US seller commission) you'd keep meaningfully more. For premium pieces, cross-listing isn't optional; it's the difference between a good month and a great one.

Quick answers

Does Poshmark charge a listing fee?

No. Listing is free and unlimited — you only pay when something sells.

Is there a separate payment processing fee?

No. The 20% (or $2.95) is all-inclusive. That's one of the few genuinely seller-friendly parts of the structure.

Who pays for shipping on Poshmark?

The buyer pays shipping by default through Poshmark's prepaid labels. You can offer discounted or free shipping to close a sale, but that comes out of your pocket.

Are Poshmark fees negotiable?

No — the rate is the same for every seller, new or veteran. Your only lever is pricing strategy: bundle low items, and cross-list the expensive ones.
