Stripe vs Square Fees: Which Is Cheaper?

Calculate it for your own numbersFree, no sign-up — every fee on one receipt.
Stripe Fee Calculator →Square Fee Calculator →

It depends where you sell. Online, Stripe wins: 2.9% + $0.30 vs Square's 3.3% + $0.30 — that's $0.40 saved per $100 sale. In person, it's nearly a tie: both cost exactly $2.75 on a $100 sale (Square 2.6% + $0.15, Stripe 2.7% + $0.05). So: online-heavy goes Stripe, in-person-heavy goes Square.

Online: Stripe wins clearly

$100 online saleFeeYou keep
Stripe (2.9% + $0.30)$3.20$96.80
Square (3.3% + $0.30)$3.60$96.40

$0.40 per sale doesn't sound like much until volume: 500 online sales a month = $200/month left on the table with Square.

In person: a dead heat

$100 in-person saleFee
Square (2.6% + $0.15)$2.75
Stripe (2.7% + $0.05)$2.75

Identical at $100 — and nearly identical at every amount, since Square's lower percentage and Stripe's lower fixed fee cancel out. At this point the decision isn't about price: it's about hardware, software, and which ecosystem you prefer. Square's free POS app and card reader bundle is genuinely good for counter selling.

Tip: Keyed-in cards cost more everywhere (around 3.5% + $0.15) — that's the price of typing card numbers instead of tapping. If staff key cards often, fix the reader before switching processors.

The verdict

  • Mostly online? Stripe. Cheaper on every transaction, better developer tools.
  • Mostly in person? Square — fees tie, but the POS hardware/software package is stronger.
  • Both? Many businesses run both: Stripe for the website, Square at the counter. No rule says one processor for everything.

Quick answers

Is Stripe cheaper than Square?

Online, yes — 2.9% + $0.30 beats 3.3% + $0.30 on every sale. In person they're effectively tied (~$2.75 per $100 either way).

Does Square have monthly fees?

No — both Stripe and Square are pay-as-you-go with no monthly fee on standard plans. You pay only per transaction.

Can I use Stripe online and Square in person?

Yes — plenty of businesses do exactly that. Your website and your counter don't have to share a processor.
