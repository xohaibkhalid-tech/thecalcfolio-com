Cash-Out Refinance vs Home Equity Loan: The Real Difference

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

A cash-out refinance replaces your current mortgage with a bigger one and hands you the difference in cash. A home equity loan keeps your mortgage untouched and adds a separate second loan. Refinance when you want a lower rate and cash; take the home equity loan when your current rate is worth protecting.

The 30-second version

  • Cash-out refinance: one new, bigger mortgage. One payment. You get cash at closing. Your rate and term reset.
  • Home equity loan: your mortgage stays exactly as-is, plus a second loan with its own payment. Usually a fixed rate, 5–30 year term.
  • HELOC: the third option — a revolving credit line against your equity, variable rate, draw as needed.
  • The deciding question isn't which is "better." It's: is your current mortgage rate worth keeping?

Tip: Owe $200k at 3.5% and need $40k? Refinancing the whole $240k at today's ~7% to grab that cash means paying 7% on money that was costing you 3.5%. A home equity loan keeps the cheap debt cheap.

The reset trap nobody mentions

Here's the quiet cost of a cash-out refinance: it restarts your mortgage clock. Fifteen years into a 30-year loan, you've finally reached the part where payments mostly hit principal instead of interest. Refinancing into a fresh 30-year term sends you back to the interest-heavy years — you could pay tens of thousands more in lifetime interest even at a decent rate.

A home equity loan doesn't touch your amortization schedule. Your first mortgage keeps marching toward payoff exactly as planned; the second loan is a separate, smaller timeline you can kill early without disturbing anything.

When each one wins

Cash-out refinance wins when: current rates are at or below your existing rate, you want to consolidate into a single payment, and the cash need is large (major renovation, debt consolidation). One payment, one rate, clean slate.

Home equity loan wins when: your current mortgage rate is lower than today's rates (hello, everyone who locked in 2020–2021), you need a fixed lump sum, and you want to preserve your payoff timeline. Two payments, but the big one stays cheap.

HELOC wins when: you need flexibility over time — phased renovations, an emergency buffer — rather than one lump sum. Just respect the variable rate: that low introductory number can move.

One more thing both options share: your home is the collateral. Borrow against it for appreciating uses (renovations, education) or high-interest debt payoff — not for a lifestyle the equity can't sustain.

Quick answers

How much cash can I take out?

Most lenders cap the new loan at 80% of your home's value (some go to 85–90% with mortgage insurance). Your "cash" is that cap minus what you still owe.

Is the interest tax-deductible?

Possibly, if you use the money to buy, build, or substantially improve the home securing the loan. The IRS rules are specific — check with a tax pro for your situation.

Which is faster to close?

Home equity loans and HELOCs usually close faster (2–4 weeks) with lighter paperwork. A cash-out refinance is a full mortgage origination — expect 30–60 days.

Can I do a cash-out refinance with bad credit?

It's harder and pricier. Conventional cash-out refis generally want a 620+ score; FHA options exist but come with mortgage insurance costs.
